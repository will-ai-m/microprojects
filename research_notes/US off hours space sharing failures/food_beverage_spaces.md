**Research status (as of 2026-10-25):** 13 WebSearch calls, plus one ToolSearch and one Read. No pages were fetched in full, so every fact below comes from search-result text or search summaries and is single-source until the primary page is read. [UNVERIFIED] marks a date, figure or attribution that a summary stated but no fetched page confirmed. Coverage is partial. Each key question's Gaps section lists what was not researched.

**Premise corrections to the assignment:**
- OpenTable's "34%" is not pop-up demand growth. The report says restaurants offered 34% more pop-ups, collaborations and chef's tables, while experiential dining rose 46% year over year ([OpenTable 2026 trends, PR Newswire, released 2025-11-18](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/opentable-reveals-the-top-trends-set-to-define-dining-in-2026-302618592.html)). - Feastly was acquired in December 2018 by Credible Inc., the parent of ChefsFeed, not by ChefsFeed as an operating company.
- No source uses "timeshare" for Geshmak. Its own site describes "restaurant space sharing." Several unrelated businesses share the name.
- Spacious pricing conflicts across sources ($20/day up to $199/month in one; $29 day pass and $95/month unlimited in another), probably reflecting different periods.

## Key Question 1: For each company, what was the model, footprint, funding, owner payment, and why did it stall, pivot, sell or shut?

### Takeaway
Sources confirm closures or sales for Spacious (shut December 31, 2019), KettleSpace (workspace assets sold April 2022), Kitchen United's Kroger network (closed November 2023), Nextbite (sold June 2023), Dinner Lab (shut April 2016), 2ndKitchen (acquired December 2021) and Feastly (acquired December 2018). The most frequently documented causes are parent-company retrenchment (Spacious), thin operator margins (Dinner Lab), capital shortfall (Pilotworks) and pivots away from physical sites toward software (Kitchen United, KettleSpace). Kitch, Mobile Kitchin, Josephine, Shef, EatsPlace, Eatwith and Virtual Dining Concepts were not researched beyond their names.

### Cited Findings

Spacious handled coffee and check-in, and partners covered extra utilities. ## Key Question 3: What US regulations block or complicate sharing a restaurant or café with a third-party chef?

### Takeaway
Retrieved sources confirm local health-permit requirements in San Francisco and enforcement against unpermitted pop-ups in Alameda County, California. Liquor licensing, dram shop liability, fire occupancy and zoning were not researched, so this section has no sourced findings on them.

### Cited Findings
- San Francisco (sf.gov guidance, updated August 2024 per a summary): pop-ups need health-department approval, renewed yearly. The host must already hold a health permit as a licensed food facility or catering company. Applications are due at least two weeks ahead. Operations are capped at three days a week. Listed fee starts at $235; a 2018 report cited $191. The two figures conflict and were not reconciled: [sf.gov](https://sf.gov/node/10266)
- Alameda County (2018 reports): an inspector halted a pop-up dinner mid-service and issued a cease-and-desist. The department cited the California Retail Food Code and held that only the licensed operator at the address could run a food business there: [SF Chronicle](https://preview-prod.w.sfchronicle.com/food/restaurants/article/Alameda-County-declares-pop-up-restaurants-illegal-13175453.php); [ABC7](https://abc7news.com/alameda-county-health-department-san-francisco-pop-up-shop-restaurants-mission-chinese/4041809/)
- An Oakland pop-up restaurant was shut down by the Alameda County Health Department: [NBC Bay Area](https://nbcbayarea.com/news/local/pop-up-restaurant-is-quickly-shut-down-in-oakland/210338); [ABC7](https://abc7news.com/alameda-county-health-department-san-francisco-pop-up-shop-restaurants-mission-chinese/4041809/)
- Guest-chef nights at a permitted Alameda café needed little extra paperwork because the café was already permitted to operate until 10 p.m. Visiting cooks needed a food-handler course, which can be done online for about $15. Article is older and undated in the summary: [Patch](https://patch.com/california/alameda/shay-s-cafe-to-begin-guest-chef-dinners)

### Inferences
- In these California examples, the permitted host kitchen holds the food-facility permit, and guest food is allowed only inside that permitted operation. This is consistent with SF's host-permit requirement and Alameda's "licensed operator at the address" position. It is my reading of the examples, not a rule quoted from code.
- Per-event approval plus annual renewal (SF) means a recurring guest-chef schedule depends on the host's permit standing.

### Gaps
- Not retrieved: NYC (DOHMH permits and commissary rules; SLA liquor license and temporary or special-event permits); California ABC rules on licensee responsibility and third-party sales; Texas TABC; Washington LCB; Illinois; DC ABRA.
- Not retrieved: dram shop liability, fire occupancy limits, zoning and use.
- Enforcement actions outside Alameda County and Oakland (2018-era): none retrieved for NYC, TX, WA, IL or DC.
- No sourced finding on who may sell alcohol under a host's license.

## Key Question 4: How big and reliable is demand from pop-up chefs and supper clubs, what can they pay, and what deal structures exist?

### Takeaway
Only platform-level consumer data was retrieved. OpenTable's late-2025 report says experiential dining bookings rose 46% year over year, and restaurants offered 34% more pop-ups, collaborations and chef's tables. No retrieved source gives pop-up chef revenue, ability to pay, or typical deal terms.

### Cited Findings
- OpenTable 2026 Dining Trends (released 2025-11-18): experiential dining up 46% year over year; restaurants offered 34% more pop-ups, collaborations and chef's tables; 48% of Americans say a unique experience makes them more likely to book; group dining up 11%; "Notify Me" alerts up 84%: [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/opentable-reveals-top-trends-set-define-dining-2026-2025-11-18); [OpenTable](https://www.opentable.com/c/top-restaurants/dining-trends/)
- OpenTable's own example: Momofuku Noodle Bar East Village ran a three-chef collaboration series, and all sessions sold out: [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/opentable-reveals-the-top-trends-set-to-define-dining-in-2026-302618592.html)
- OpenTable describes the figures as its own platform data and surveys, with 2026 figures largely projections: [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/opentable-reveals-the-top-trends-set-to-define-dining-in-2026-302618592.html)
- Deal structures seen: Spacious revenue share (percentage of membership revenue, scaled to check-ins); Geshmak's company claim that it may fund a guest's first food order; Feastly as a marketplace for chef-hosted events (fee terms not found): see the links in Key Question 1.
- Sugar Studio cited rising ingredient, labor and utility costs (April 2026), a sign of cost pressure on small food operators: [Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/04/west-allis-sugar-studio-bakery-folds-under-crushing-costs/)

### Inferences
- OpenTable's 34% measures restaurant-side supply, not guest-chef demand. The 46% figure measures consumer booking interest in experiential dining. The guest-chef market overlaps with that population, but the overlap is not measured.
- Report language should use the supply-side wording, not "pop-ups up 34%."

### Gaps
- Not found: pop-up chef and supper-club revenue, ability to pay, typical flat fee versus revenue share versus minimum spend.
- Not found: reliability data (cancellations, churn), count of US pop-up operators, Eatwith US economics.

## Key Question 5: Why did virtual-brand and ghost-kitchen models collapse in 2022–2024?

### Takeaway
Sources describe layoffs, brand shutdowns and consumer disinterest, not one collapse event. The documented causes are weak brand consistency and quality, little real customer connection, restaurants refocusing on core menus, and operator capital problems. Delivery-app fee economics, customer backlash and operational strain are not sourced here.

### Cited Findings
- Nextbite had a third layoff round in about 14 months before its June 2023 sale. It sold Ordermark to UrbanPiper and was acquired by Sam Nazarian: [Restaurant Dive](https://restaurantdive.com/news/Nextbite-sells-Ordermark-US-to-Urbanpiper/652643); [NRN](https://www.nrn.com/restaurant-technology/nextbite-acquired-by-sbe-hospitality-group-ceo-sam-nazarian)
- Red Robin stopped virtual brands including MrBeast Burger, to focus on its core menu. Brinker shut Maggiano's Italian Classics in May (year not stated in the summary; likely 2023): [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/red-robin-stops-selling-mrbeast-burger-chicken-sammys-wing-dept-fresh-set/687537/)
- Euromonitor's Dorothy Calba said most virtual-brand trouble "centered around the issue of consistency and quality." She also said consumers were dining out again and virtual brands never built a real connection with them: [Restaurant Dive, "virtual brand troubles are growing pains"](https://restaurantdive.com/news/virtual-brand-troubles-are-growing-pains-not-death-rattle-experts-say/696216)
- Counterview in the same coverage: experts called the turmoil common in emerging markets, and one operator called the segment "the second inning of a nine-inning ballgame": [Restaurant Dive](https://restaurantdive.com/news/virtual-brand-troubles-are-growing-pains-not-death-rattle-experts-say/696216)
- Restaurant Dive reader coverage, headlined "virtual brands are too complex and have low sales." Readers unanimously expected no growth in prevalence over the next year. This is opinion, not market data: [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/readers-respond-virtual-brands-are-too-complex-and-have-low-sales/689464/)
- Kitchen United shut its Kroger units in November 2023 and pivoted toward software: [Supermarket News](https://www.supermarketnews.com/independents-regional-grocers/kitchen-united-closes-all-kroger-locations-as-company-shifts-to-software-focus)
- Headlines only, bodies not retrieved: "The Great Ghost Kitchen Collapse Is Here" ([The Takeout](https://www.thetakeout.com:443/ghost-kitchens-in-2023-vs-pandemic-delivery-era-closing-1851077823)); "Ghost Kitchens Vanish as In-Person Dining Returns," 2024 ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/restaurant-technology/2024/ghost-kitchens-vanish-as-in-person-dining-returns/))

### Inferences
- Several sources link the decline to consumers returning to in-person dining, which reduced delivery demand for pandemic-era virtual brands. The headline-level framing supports this, but I did not read the full articles.
- The brand-quality and consistency complaints suggest the product, not spare kitchen capacity, was the binding constraint.

### Gaps
- Delivery-app commission and fee data for 2022–2024: not retrieved.
- Customer backlash evidence and operational strain (staffing, throughput): not retrieved.
- Ghost-kitchen closure counts or market size for 2022–2024: not retrieved.
- Virtual Dining Concepts funding and status: not retrieved.
- Body text of NRN's "Nextbite's failures" analysis: not retrieved.
