# US Legal, Regulatory, Insurance, Lease, and Tax Barriers to Renting Commercial Space During the Owner's Off Hours

Research date: 2026-10-25. Scope: United States only. This is research, not legal advice. Rules vary sharply by city and state, and most facts below come from one jurisdiction.

**How to read this file.** Sources are labeled [Primary] (government pages, court or docket records, platform terms, agency forms) or [Secondary] (news, bankruptcy-alert sites, law-firm and broker blogs, trade press). Almost everything was taken from search-result summaries. No statute, ordinance, court opinion, or platform terms document was read in full. The one direct fetch (Peerspace property damage guarantee terms) failed with a DNS error (ENOTFOUND) and was not retried. Items marked "single source" should be treated as unverified. Research used 16 tool calls.

## 1. Zoning, Certificate of Occupancy, Fire Code, and Event Permits

### Takeaway
A commercial building's certificate of occupancy (CO) fixes its legal use and capacity. Renting the space for events, and especially for alcohol, live entertainment, dancing, or crowds, can require a separate place-of-assembly sign-off, a fire permit, or a discretionary conditional use permit (CUP). These are per-property gates, not per-booking ones, so software and insurance cannot clear them. The evidence in this pass covers NYC and Los Angeles. Chicago, San Francisco, and Texas were not researched.

### Cited Findings
- NYC requires a Place of Assembly Certificate of Operation (PACO) from DOB for a space used for assembly. Per the DOB page summary, a first PACO follows inspector sign-off and lasts one year. A separate FDNY permit, also one year, is needed to operate, with a mandatory annual FDNY inspection by default. The CO sets the posted capacity before the space opens. [Primary] — [NYC DOB PACO page](https://home4.nyc.gov/site/buildings/dob/project-categories-paco.page) (content from search summary, not read in full, as of 2026-10-25)
- NYC enforcement example: a Brooklyn bar (Saint Vitus) was reported operating without a PACO. Its last certificate dated to 1953 and described a commercial store and machinery storage use. Administrative adjudicators sustained the violation, and the owner paid a $1,250 fine. A separate Metropolitan Avenue event venue held a certificate that still described a hardware store, and DOB had received no change-of-use request. [Secondary; single source] — [Greenpointers](https://greenpointers.com/?p=141853) (URL matched by search; not confirmed as the case source)
- NYC city-owned venues: DCAS handles rental requests for DCAS-managed buildings. Applications are due about six weeks before the event. The 2021 CECM guide sets a daily surcharge of $1,000 for private renters and $500 for nonprofits, triggered when setup or unfinished removal keeps the room unusable for adjacent days. [Primary] — [NYC CECM Event Permitting Guide 2021](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/cecm/downloads/pdf/NYC_CECM_Comprehensive_Event_Permitting_Guide_2021.pdf); [DCAS event space rental](https://www.nyc.gov/site/dcas/business/event-space-rental.page)
- Los Angeles CUP practice: alcohol sales, live entertainment, and dancing at banquet and event venues typically go through a Class 2 CUP with hour limits. Example: a 2026 Central Area Planning Commission determination for a 7,056 sq ft banquet hall in a C2-1 zone, requesting alcohol sales, live entertainment, dancing, and hours to 2 a.m., decided under LAMC 12.24 W.1 with the appeal partly granted. [Primary; document identity not fully confirmed] — [LA City Planning case document](https://planning.lacity.gov/dcpapi2/meetings/document/80513)
- Other LA-area examples: an unincorporated LA County banquet hall CUP capped occupancy at 152 and relied on leased off-site parking [Primary] — [LA County case record](https://case.planning.lacounty.gov/case/view/project_no._r2007-02619-1_conditional_use_permit_200700178_parking_permit_c). Lancaster's banquet CUP was delayed over crowd control, security, and alcoholic beverage control concerns [Primary] — [City of Lancaster document](https://cityoflancasterca.org/home/showpublisheddocument/13908/635775792210230000) (document identity not confirmed).
- Generic CO trigger: one municipal code provision (jurisdiction not identified) requires a CO before a new building is occupied, an existing building is altered, or a building's use changes. [Primary; jurisdiction unknown] — [ecode360 provision](https://ecode360.com/50601371)

### Inferences
- The NYC examples show that enforcement attaches to the space's CO history, not only to individual events. A retail, storage, or office CO used for assembly is the typical exposure. (Inference from the two NYC cases.)
- An LA-style CUP adds hearings, conditions, and months of lead time, and it is a property-level approval. It cannot be cleared per booking. (Inference from LA case records.)
- Any model that lets bookings go live without verifying the CO or permitted use is exposed in NYC-type regimes. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- Chicago, San Francisco, and Texas city rules were not researched, although the brief asked for them.
- IBC/IFC assembly occupancy and change-of-occupancy text was not retrieved. Occupant-load tables were not read.
- Special event street or sidewalk permits and noise ordinances were not researched.
- No source found on whether a private venue's after-hours events trigger a specific CO change in any city.

## 2. Premises Liability, Insurance, Platform Guarantees, and Section 230

### Takeaway
Injury liability at a booked event is spread among the owner, the renter or host, and the platform. Platform cover is typically per-booking, capped, and expressly not a substitute for the host's own insurance. Platform immunity is contested. Trial courts have dismissed some claims against Airbnb in party-shooting cases, but a San Francisco Superior Court judge let a negligence case in a fatal house-party shooting proceed. No appellate Section 230 holding for an event-space platform was found. Peerspace filed Chapter 11 in August 2026 while a serious-injury premises case against it remained pending.

### Cited Findings

**Peerspace (platform cover)**
- Peerspace host pages for Canada and the UK (and AU, per search) advertise up to $1M per booking in supplemental general liability included with each booking. The US host page was not captured directly. [Primary; platform marketing; content from search summary] — [Peerspace CA host page](https://www.peerspace.com/ca/host); [Peerspace UK host page](https://www.peerspace.com/uk/host)
- Peerspace's Property Damage Guarantee covers up to $25,000 per booking for damage caused by guests. The summary says it is available for US-based venues only, and that it "does not constitute an insurance policy" and does not reduce the host's obligation to carry its own insurance. [Primary; terms not read directly; fetch failed] — [Peerspace Property Damage Guarantee Terms](https://www.peerspace.com/legal/terms/property-damage-guarantee)
- Peerspace does not insure guests directly, so guests and hosts both need their own primary coverage for the booked activity. [Primary; help-center article; article ID not confirmed] — [Peerspace help center](https://intercom.help/peerspace-108cf19aff8e/en/articles/10119116)
- **Conflict on claim window:** Peerspace's help article says damage must be reported within 3 days after the event. A third-party site, shunins.com, says 48 hours and "up to $1M per claim," which conflicts with Peerspace's per-booking wording. [Secondary] — [shunins.com](https://shunins.com/article/does-peerspace-have-insurance). Treat the platform's own terms as controlling, once read.
- A third-party review says the $1M liability cover does not reach theft or accidental breakage and recommends a deposit or added property cover. [Secondary; single source; attribution uncertain] — [sidehusl.com Peerspace review](https://sidehusl.com/peerspace/); [Thimble](https://www.thimble.com/?p=33945)

**Peerspace (litigation and bankruptcy)**
- Reynaga case: Ezequiel Reynaga, 31, fell down a staircase at a Chicago residence in July 2024 and was left paraplegic. He sued Peerspace and the space's owners and operators in Cook County Circuit Court (negligence and premises liability). All other defendants settled. Per the summary, trial was set for August 31, 2026, and settlement talks between Peerspace and Reynaga had not succeeded. [Secondary; bankruptcy-alert summary, not a court record] — [Bondoro case summary](https://app.bondoro.com/preview/cases/Peerspace_Inc/case-summary)
- Peerspace, Inc. filed Chapter 11 on August 30, 2026, in the Northern District of California (Case No. 26-30766), under Subchapter V, before Judge Dennis Montali. The 341 meeting was October 5, 2026, and the claims bar date is November 9, 2026. Per a company statement reported in the press, the filing resolves pending litigation and does not reflect operations; the company expects no effect on listings, bookings, or host payouts. [Secondary; aggregators; verify on PACER] — [Inforuptcy docket page](https://www.inforuptcy.com/browse-filings/california-northern-bankruptcy-court/3:26-bk-30766/bankruptcy-case-peerspace-inc); [Bondoro filing alert](https://app.bondoro.com/preview/cases/Peerspace_Inc); [WhatNow](https://whatnow.com/news/trending/san-francisco-venue-rental-marketplace-with-45000-spaces-files-for-chapter-11-bankruptcy/). The Reynaga and bankruptcy sources agree on the August 2026 timing.

**Airbnb (party-house shootings and platform defenses)**
- *Elhania v. Airbnb* (San Francisco Superior Court, reported around August 2024): the judge rejected Airbnb's bid to dismiss negligence and unfair-competition claims arising from a fatal house-party shooting. Plaintiffs argue Airbnb violated a local short-term rental ordinance. [Secondary; ruling not read] — [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12001736/case-against-airbnb-in-fatal-house-party-shooting-can-proceed-judge-rules); [SF Chronicle](https://www.sfchronicle.com/bayarea/article/judge-allows-lawsuit-airbnb-homicide-move-forward-19717962.php); [Daily Journal](https://dailyjournal.com/article/380490-no-dismissal-of-case-seeking-to-hold-airbnb-liable-for-shooting-death)
- *Jackson v. Airbnb* (C.D. Cal., November 2022): claims against Airbnb were dismissed after a shooting at an Airbnb rental. Per Eric Goldman's blog, the Snap claims were dismissed under Section 230, while the Airbnb dismissal rested on the elements of the claims rather than Section 230. [Secondary; expert blog; single source; opinion not read] — [Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2022/11/snap-and-airbnb-arent-liable-for-tragic-shooting-jackson-v-airbnb.htm)
- Massachusetts SJC (June 2021): affirmed no liability for a homeowner in a party guest's shooting death. It declined to treat a short-term host as a common carrier or place of public accommodation. [Secondary; single source; holding not verified against the opinion] — [Savory Tort blog](https://www.thesavorytort.com/2021/06/no-duty-court-clears-homeowner-of.html?m=1)
- Bath Township, Ohio (2025 mass shooting): Airbnb's filing denies zoning violations and negligence and asks the court to dismiss, arguing the injuries were caused by people and circumstances beyond its control. [Secondary; filing month reported as July, year not confirmed] — [Hotel-Online](https://www.hotel-online.com/news/airbnb-denies-zoning-violations-negligence-in-lawsuit-over-ohio-s-2025-bath-township-mass-shooting)
- Orinda, California (Halloween house-party shooting): the victim's family alleges Airbnb should have known about large crowds, underage drinking, and illegal activity. The complaint cites local occupancy rules, and the owners had been cited multiple times. [Secondary; trade press] — [ShortTermRentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/airbnb-halloween-shooting-lawsuit/)
- Airbnb reportedly committed to banning party houses and adding guest standards. [Secondary; single source; date and policy text not verified] — [ShortTermRentalz](https://shorttermrentalz.com/news/airbnb-court-california-shooting-party/)
- Non-US context: a Toronto shooting victim sought $5 million from Airbnb, the homeowner, the renters, and the shooter. [Secondary; Canadian] — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.5377498)

**Venue and host insurance requirements (government examples)**
- Roseville, CA: liquor liability is required only when alcohol is provided, at $1M per occurrence and $2M aggregate. [Primary] — [City of Roseville meeting space](https://www.roseville.ca.us/meetingspace)
- Cupertino, CA: groups serving alcohol need host liquor coverage, name the city as additional insured, and deliver the certificate 30 days before the rental. [Primary; document identity not confirmed] — [Cupertino document](https://cupertino.org/home/showpublisheddocument/29237/637546749852300000)
- Alaska (state property): liquor coverage is triggered at 75 or more guests, or whenever alcohol is sold or served. [Primary] — [Alaska LAM rental insurance](https://lam.alaska.gov/rental/insurance)
- Broker view: host liquor coverage applies when alcohol is complimentary; retail liquor coverage is added when alcohol is sold. [Secondary] — [Insurance Canopy venue checklist](https://www.insurancecanopy.com/blog/event-host-venue-insurance-checklist)

**Swimply (platform cover)**
- Swimply's host coverage runs through Proper Insurance. Proper's marketing says its policy covers guest-caused damage, liquor liability, and amenity liability for pools and similar features. [Insurer marketing] — [Proper Insurance](https://www.proper.insure/blog/swimply-insurance-coverage-for-hosts)
- A third-party review says every host gets up to $1M in liability and up to $10,000 in property-damage protection, and that the cover is not a guarantee against all liability. Guests also sign waivers. [Secondary; single source for the figures] — [Clark.com](https://clark.com/make-money/swimply/amp/)
- A news report says Swimply spent two years working with insurers to get a $1M host policy. [Secondary; single source] — [NewsNational via 10News](https://www.10news.com/news/national/people-are-renting-out-their-backyard-pools-how-safe-is-it)

### Inferences
- Section 230 and "marketplace, not operator" defenses are partial and fact-specific. The sources show platforms winning some dismissals (Jackson; the Massachusetts host case, which is not a platform case) and losing others (Elhania). Where a plaintiff pleads that the platform itself set the rules, pricing, or local-law compliance, negligence and ordinance-based claims survived at the pleading stage. (Inference from the case list. No appellate Section 230 ruling was found.)
- Per-booking $1M general liability is aimed at ordinary premises claims. Its fit with violent-incident tail risk depends on exclusions, which were not read (see Gaps). The platform's own description of its guarantee as "not insurance" puts the tail risk back on the host and guest.
- Peerspace's bankruptcy means that a claimant's recovery from a platform depends on its solvency and on how insurance proceeds are allocated in the case. This is the first main practical point for any host or guest injured at a booked event. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- No primary policy document was read. Carrier, named insureds, limits per claim versus per booking, and exclusions (assault and battery, liquor, unlisted activities) are unverified for Peerspace and Swimply. Fetch of Peerspace's terms failed.
- The Peerspace claim-window conflict (3 days versus 48 hours) is unresolved.
- Current status of Reynaga and of the Peerspace bankruptcy after the August 2026 filing is unknown beyond the aggregator facts above. Check the Cook County and N.D. Cal. dockets.
- Section 230 holdings were not read in opinion form. The basis of the Elhania ruling is unclear from the sources.
- Airbnb's party-ban date and terms were not verified.
- How insurers treat short-term commercial renters (underwriting, excess cover, hired and non-owned auto, exclusions for event occupancy) was not researched.
- How Facilitron, SpotHero, ResortPass, and Giggster handle insurance was not researched.

## 3. Commercial Leases: Use Clauses, Sublease, Assignment, and Licenses

### Takeaway
Leases typically restrict permitted use and hours, require landlord consent to sublease or assign, and require the tenant to carry insurance. Renting out a space after hours is usually a sublease or license question, and the use clause is often the bigger blocker: a lease that bars a use (for example, assembly, alcohol, or entertainment) bars it regardless of how the rental is structured. This pass did not find a source showing that a platform "license" structure avoids sublease restrictions. That is a legal conclusion for counsel.

### Cited Findings
- The use clause can limit the business type and the permitted hours. Changing the permitted use can require landlord approval or local-authority consent. [Secondary; law-firm commentary] — [Mondaq, key commercial lease clauses](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/landlord-tenant-leases/1138752/understanding-key-clauses-in-your-commercial-lease-agreement)
- A sublease clause governs whether the tenant can rent all or part of its space to someone else. NYC practice, as described by a brokerage site, treats partial short-term arrangements as licenses. [Secondary; practice description, not statute; single source] — [Metro Manhattan, subleasing and assignment](https://www.metro-manhattan.com/subleasing-assignment/)
- A sublease usually requires landlord consent, and the sublease must align with the master lease. The sub-lessor remains bound by the master lease. [Secondary; law-firm commentary; jurisdiction not specified] — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/can-you-sublease-commercial-property-without-consent/)
- Landlords can build in rights to reject certain industries or weak subtenants, to share sublease profit, and to recapture space. [Secondary; from the same search results; attribution to a specific source not confirmed] — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/can-you-sublease-commercial-property-without-consent/); [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/landlord-tenant-leases/1138752/understanding-key-clauses-in-your-commercial-lease-agreement)
- Example from the same commentary: a medical tenant cannot sublet space for showroom use, because a sub-lessor cannot violate the use clause. [Secondary] — [NDV Law](https://ndvlaw.com/can-you-sublease-commercial-property-without-consent/)

### Inferences
- The use clause is probably the first gate. An office, medical, or retail lease that does not permit assembly or entertainment will block after-hours rental even if the rental is structured as a license. (Inference from the use-clause and sublease sources.)
- Whether a "license" avoids sublease consent depends on the lease-versus-license test, which turns on exclusive possession and control. That test was not sourced here, so the claim cannot be confirmed from this research.

### Gaps
- No source on the lease-versus-license test, hourly or coworking-style license agreements, or case law on when a platform's license counts as a sublease. Follow-up queries: "license vs lease occupancy test," "coworking license commercial lease prohibited sublease."
- Landlord insurance requirements (additional-insured status, limits, certificates) in commercial leases were not researched.
- Whether revenue-sharing with a platform triggers landlord profit-share or recapture rights was not confirmed beyond the general commentary above.

## 4. Alcohol: ABC/SLA Licensing, Event Permits, BYOB, and Dram Shop

### Takeaway
Alcohol is the most structural constraint found so far. In California, a for-profit or individual event organizer generally cannot hold a one-day ABC license. The workable routes are a nonprofit licensee with local police sign-off and a certified server, a licensed caterer, or the venue's own license plus local approvals. Texas (TABC), New York (SLA), BYOB rules, and dram shop liability were not researched.

### Cited Findings
- San Francisco guidance: a one-day license cannot go to a for-profit business or individual organizer. Those organizers need a caterer holding a Type 58 permit. [Primary; local government] — [SF.gov, serve alcohol at an outdoor special event](https://www.sf.gov/serve-alcohol-at-your-outdoor-special-event)
- Palo Alto ABC FAQ: a registered 501(c)(3) can apply directly for a one-day event permit (ABC-221). [Primary; local government] — [Palo Alto ABC FAQ](https://www.paloalto.gov/files/0bc183fc-6092-464c-a011-62f756665212/ABC-FAQ.pdf)
- ABC-221 process (per the form instructions, read as summary only): local police or sheriff approval is required, either by letter or by signature on the form. The form must be received within 10 days of the event and should not be filed more than 30 days ahead. [Primary; state form instructions; summary only] — [ABC-221 instructions](https://abc.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/forms/ABC-221%20Instr.pdf)
- Responsible beverage service: per a search summary of the form, nonprofits have needed a certified server on site since July 2022, with server details listed on the ABC-221. Mountain House describes the server as needed at all times. The timing requirement is inconsistent across the sources and needs confirming with ABC. [Secondary summary; single source on timing] — [ABC-221 instructions](https://abc.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/forms/ABC-221%20Instr.pdf)
- Pleasanton: when a business hosts a nonprofit's fundraiser, the nonprofit must apply for the one-day license for the business location, and the licensing burden sits with the nonprofit. [Primary; local] — [Pleasanton police alcohol waiver letter](https://cityofpleasantonca.gov/assets/our-government/police/alcohol%20waiver%20letter.pdf)
- Fees (2024 event toolkit, Sacramento): $50 per day for beer and wine, $75 with spirits, $100 for a special temporary license. Figures may have changed. [Secondary; dated 2024] — [Downtown Sacramento General Event Toolkit](https://www.downtownsac.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/General-Event-Toolkit.pdf)
- Venue-level alcohol approvals also trigger local CUPs in LA (see Section 1) and liquor insurance requirements in several cities (see Section 2).

### Inferences
- In California, a commercial platform-hosted event serving alcohol generally needs a licensed caterer or the venue's own license. A nonprofit one-day license does not solve it for a commercial organizer. (Inference from the SF, Palo Alto, and Pleasanton guidance. State-level verification needed.)
- Alcohol is the most likely event category to trigger a local CUP, local police sign-off, and a liquor insurance endorsement at once. (Inference from the LA and Pleasanton examples.)

### Gaps
- Dram shop (commercial host) liability by state was not researched. No source was found for this section.
- Texas TABC event or temporary permits, New York SLA one-time permits, and BYOB rules were not researched.
- ABC fee schedule and the current RBS timing rule were not verified against ABC's own pages.

## 5. Food Service: Health Permits, Commissary and Shared Kitchens, Guest Chefs, Cottage Food

### Takeaway
Food service needs a permitted kitchen. Caterers and temporary vendors commonly must use a commissary, and a shared or host kitchen usually needs its own plan review, written agreement, or per-operator permit. A visiting chef in someone else's kitchen therefore needs the host kitchen's permit to cover the menu, or a permit of their own. Rules vary by county. Cottage food limits and NYC, LA, SF, Chicago, and Texas guest-chef rules were not researched.

### Cited Findings
- Tacoma-Pierce County (WA): a commissary is a permitted facility where food is prepared, stored, or packaged but not served on site. Caterers, mobile vendors, and temporary event vendors must use one. Shared use is allowed, but the kitchen needs its own permit, with plan review and final approval before operators use it. [Primary; local health department] — [TPCHD](https://tpchd.org/?p=377)
- Skagit County (WA): commissary kitchens must have adequate sinks, cooking equipment, and refrigeration for clients' menus. Out-of-state license holders need approval from the local health department. [Primary; local] — [Skagit County commissary info, Nov 2023](https://skagitcounty.net/HealthFood/Documents/Commissary%20-%20Info%202023.11.pdf)
- Virginia: where a caterer operates from a shared commissary, both the commissary kitchen and the caterer need separate permits. [Primary; state health department] — [VDH, applying for a new foodservice facility](https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/blue-ridge/applying-for-new-foodservice-facilities/)
- King County (WA): permitted restaurants do not need a separate catering permit if catering takes place inside the existing permitted kitchen, and the kitchen owner must sign a shared-kitchen agreement. [Primary; local] — [King County catering permit](https://kingcounty.gov/en/dept/dph/certificates-permits-licenses/food-business-permits/catering-home-based-business-permit)
- San Francisco: caterers must file a commissary or permitted-kitchen verification form. [Primary; local form, Oct 2023 version] — [SF commissary verification form](https://sf.gov/sites/default/files/2023-10/Commissary%20or%20Permitted%20Kitchen%20Verification%20Form%20for%20Caterers%20-%20CommVerification.pdf)

### Inferences
- The host kitchen's permit probably does not cover a visiting chef's menu without the host's written agreement and the health department's sign-off. (Inference from the King County and Virginia rules, which are in different jurisdictions.)
- A verified-caterer or verified-commissary model, as in the SF form, is the workable pattern for a platform. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- No guest-chef or pop-up rule was found for NYC, LA, SF, Chicago, or Texas.
- Cottage food limits, which vary by state, were not researched.
- Mobile food unit and event-vendor permits were not researched beyond the commissary requirement.

## 6. Taxes: Occupancy and Sales Tax, 1099-K, Marketplace Facilitator, UBIT, Property Tax

### Takeaway
Three points are sourced. (a) The 1099-K reporting threshold is back at $20,000 and 200 transactions after the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), which narrows platform reporting. (b) Occupancy-tax codes are written for lodging, and the sources found do not say whether they reach hourly rental of event space. (c) Income remains taxable whether or not a 1099-K is issued. Marketplace-facilitator rules, sales tax on venue rentals, and nonprofit UBIT and property-tax risks were not researched.

### Cited Findings
- OBBBA (signed July 4, 2025) reinstated the pre-ARPA 1099-K threshold. The IRS issued FAQs and a news release (IR-2025-107, October 23, 2025) saying the dollar limit reverted to $20,000. [Primary; IRS] — [IRS FAQ on the 1099-K threshold](https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000)
- Under the restored rule, a filer must exceed both $20,000 in gross payments and 200 transactions. [Primary per IRS FAQ; figures read from a search summary] — [IRS FAQ](https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-issues-faqs-on-form-1099-k-threshold-under-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-dollar-limit-reverts-to-20000); [TaxAct explainer](https://blog.taxact.com/new-form-1099-k-reporting-thresholds)
- **Conflict on effective date:** one source says the change reaches tax years beginning in 2022; an AI-generated summary of an IRS webinar says retroactive to 2021; Credit Karma ties it to payments after 2024. The IRS fact sheet should be checked before relying on any date. [Secondary; sources conflict] — [Carry](https://carry.com/news/irs-reverts-1099-k-reporting-threshold-20000); [CreditKarma](https://www.creditkarma.com/tax/library/irs-forms/1099-k-threshold/)
- An IRS official cautioned that income is taxable even if no 1099-K is issued. [Secondary; attribution to the official not confirmed] — [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/daily-tax-report/irs-releases-details-on-form-1099-k-changes-from-gop-tax-law)
- Austin, TX: a hotel is a building offering sleeping accommodations for 30 days or less. [Primary; municipal code/definition] — [Austin hotel tax definition](https://www.austintexas.gov/node/14931)
- Waco, TX: the tax reaches occupants of "any room or space furnished by any hotel." Whether "space" reaches event rental is not addressed. [Primary; local] — [Waco hotel occupancy tax](https://www.waco-texas.com/Departments/Budget-Finance/Hotel-Occupancy-Taxes)
- Kerrville, TX: collection duty covers guests who rent a room or space in a hotel. [Primary; local] — [Kerrville hotel occupancy tax](https://kerrvilletx.gov/1975/Hotel-Occupancy-Tax)
- Torrance, CA: short-term rental means stays of 30 consecutive calendar days or less. [Primary; local] — [City of Torrance taxes](https://torranceca.gov/government/city-departments/finance/fees-fines-and-taxes/taxes)
- Austin's 2% venue project tax applies to hotel charges subject to the tax. It is not a separate tax on event rentals. [Primary; local] — [Austin hotel and rental tax](https://www.austintexas.gov/financial-services/hotel-and-rental-tax)

### Inferences
- A 30-day ceiling in the lodging definition probably does not exclude hourly rentals on duration alone, but the outcome turns on how each local code defines "hotel" and "space." (Inference from the Torrance and Austin definitions. Not stated in any source.)
- Occupancy-tax exposure for event venues looks plausible in some jurisdictions and unresolved in others. A platform collection model would solve it, but no source here confirms how platforms actually handle this.

### Gaps
- Marketplace-facilitator laws (sales and occupancy tax collection by platforms) were not researched.
- Sales tax on venue or meeting-space rentals in states that tax rentals of tangible property or amusements was not researched.
- Nonprofit UBIT and property-tax-exemption risks from renting space to third parties were not researched.
- Host-side 1099-NEC or 1099-MISC obligations for platform payouts were not researched.
- New York City, Los Angeles, San Francisco, and Chicago occupancy-tax treatment of event space was not researched.

- ADA Title III obligations for a space that opens to paying third-party events (accessible routes, restrooms, parking, and similar). Confirm whether the owner or the renter bears them under the lease and the law.

## 8. Deal-Breakers Versus Solvable Barriers, and How Platforms Have Responded

### Takeaway
Some barriers are structural and per-property: the certificate of occupancy and permitted use, the lease use clause, and California's alcohol rules for commercial organizers. Software and insurance do not clear them. Other barriers can be managed per booking: liability and damage cover, licensed caterers, food-permit verification, and 1099-K reporting. The residual tail risk of a serious injury or violent incident remains only partly covered, and the platform's own defenses are contested.

### Cited Findings (platform responses)
- Peerspace: per-booking supplemental general liability up to $1M (per host pages for CA, UK, and AU; US page not captured) and a damage guarantee up to $25,000 per booking, US only, described as not insurance (see Section 2). Peerspace filed Chapter 11 on August 30, 2026 while a premises injury case was pending (see Section 2). [Primary and secondary as labeled in Section 2]
- Swimply: partnership with Proper Insurance; third-party figures of $1M liability and $10,000 property damage (see Section 2). [Insurer marketing; secondary]
- Airbnb: reported party-house ban and new guest standards (single source, undated); defends shooting suits on negligence, zoning, and causation grounds (see Section 2). [Secondary]
- Facilitron, SpotHero, ResortPass: not researched (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Platforms have converged on four responses: (1) per-booking liability cover, (2) capped damage guarantees explicitly described as not insurance, (3) bans or restrictions on high-risk event types such as parties, and (4) pushing alcohol and food compliance onto licensed caterers, nonprofit licensees, or verified kitchens. None of these removes the per-property gates in Sections 1 and 3, and none is shown to cover the full tail of violent-incident liability. (Inference from Sections 2, 4, and 5.)
- Likely deal-breakers for a "rent my space after hours" model: a CO or permitted use that does not cover the event (NYC-type PACO regimes, LA-type CUPs); a lease use clause that bars the use; and commercial alcohol service in California without a licensed caterer or venue license. (Inference from Sections 1, 3, and 4.)
- Likely solvable with process: per-booking liability and damage cover, caterer and kitchen verification, 1099-K handling, and event-type restrictions. Their effectiveness is unproven without the policy wording. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- How Facilitron, SpotHero, ResortPass, Giggster, and Airbnb Experiences handle insurance, permits, and alcohol was not researched.
- No loss data, claim frequency, or insurer underwriting positions were found for short-term commercial renters.
- Whether Peerspace's per-booking cover applies to violent-incident claims depends on policy exclusions, which were not read.
- No source was found showing how platforms verify host CO, lease consent, or local permits at booking.
