# US Short-Term Retail and Event-Venue Marketplaces (excluding Peerspace): Failures, Consolidation, Survivors

Research date: 2026-10-25. Method: WebSearch only (about 15 calls; WebFetch was not used, per the environment note). Every fact below comes from a search-result snippet or summary; no page was opened in full. Treat each claim as "as reported in search results" until a primary source is read. Dates are labeled per fact. Nothing dated after 2024 was found except the Splacer closure report (June 2025, updated May 2026), so October 2026 developments are not covered.

Source tags: [primary] = company press release, court filing, or company-authored page. [trade] = trade or business press. [agg] = aggregator or database profile (CB Insights, Dealroom, startup databases, KingsCrowd). [lead] = mentioned in a search summary with no retrieved URL; unverified, so it sits in Gaps.

## KQ1. Per-company facts: model, US markets, funding, scale, take rate, failure, pivot, acquisition

### Takeaway
Of the companies checked, the clearest failures are retail operators (b8ta, US ops ended Feb 2022; Showfields, Chapter 11 Oct 2023; Neighborhood Goods, all stores closed Jan 2024), and the event marketplaces were absorbed by software or restaurant-tech buyers (EventUp to Gather, then Tripleseat; VenueBook to Seated, Aug 2020). Splacer is reported closed (June 2025) on database profiles alone. Storefront, Appear Here US, and Bulletin have no confirmed status after 2018 (Bulletin) or 2016 (Storefront merger). Giggster was still active in 2023 (acquisition). Venuetastic is inactive with no date found.

### Inferences
- Event marketplaces were absorbed. EventUp went to Gather, then Tripleseat (2018 to 2020). VenueBook went to Seated (2020). Both buyers were software or restaurant-tech platforms, not marketplaces. The marketplace became a feature of a venue or restaurant workflow product.
- Retail operators that carried fixed lease or store-operating costs failed after traffic or landlord shocks (b8ta 2022; Showfields 2023; Neighborhood Goods 2024). Each cited leases or landlords, so the lease burden looks central. This pattern rests on about three cases with self-described causes.
- Take rates differ by vertical. Event bookings in the 2014 to 2015 era: Splacer reportedly about 15% (lead, unverified). Retail sharing in 2017: fixed rent plus 30% of sales (Bulletin). Retail took a larger share of revenue, though the years and samples differ.

## KQ2. Supply-side barriers for retail (lease provisions, landlord reluctance, brokers)

### Takeaway
Evidence on pop-up leasing barriers comes mainly from UK, Australian, and general legal commentary, with US-specific lease clauses unverified. The most-cited issues are licence-vs-lease status and holdover risk, tenant credit risk, repeated handover and reinstatement costs, and conflicts with existing tenants' use rights. Two US cases (b8ta, Showfields) name landlord negotiations and "rigid leases" as causes.

### Cited Findings
- Pop-up occupancy is often structured as a licence rather than a lease. An occupant who stays past the end date may argue it has landlord-tenant rights and litigate for possession. Source is a 2022 CLE course title and summary; provider is US-based but the jurisdiction of content was not confirmed. [trade / legal education] [BARBRI CLE, 2022](https://www.barbri.com/course/professional-development/CLE/popup-leasing-drafting-delivery-insurance-and-maintenance-pr_2022-09-14)
- Tenant credit risk: short-term occupiers are often startups without trading history. Landlords demanding large upfront rent or deposits may deter the tenants who make the format viable. [UK legal commentary, per search summary] [Vincents Solicitors](https://vincentssolicitors.co.uk/?p=7545); [IREI](https://irei.com/publications/article/a-sudden-retail-revival-the-legal-realities-behind-pop-up-shops-and-short-term-leasing-in-the-united-kingdom/) (paywalled; only the introduction seen)
- Operational burden: each pop-up needs its own negotiation and documentation, and units may be handed over, inspected, and reinstated several times a year. [UK/AU legal commentary, per search summary] [Vincents Solicitors](https://vincentssolicitors.co.uk/?p=7545); [Australian Property Journal](https://australianpropertyjournal.com.au/?p=11091)
- Turnover and conflicts: landlords face high turnover and use restrictions that can clash with existing tenants, and an ill-suited occupier can dilute a centre's positioning. This is the closest evidence to exclusive-use and co-tenancy issues, but US clause language was not reviewed. [trade / legal, per search summary] [Australian Property Journal](https://australianpropertyjournal.com.au/?p=11091)
- Reinstatement: landlords want space returned to original condition. Strip-out costs can exceed the rent. [per search summary] [ICSC "Pop Goes Retail" PDF](https://www.icsc.com/uploads/event_documents/Pop_Goes_Retail_(RT_April_30).pdf) (listed; not opened)
- Insurance: landlords usually insure the building. Stock, fittings, and public liability fall to the occupier, and tenants who assume the landlord's policy covers them may be exposed. [per search summary] [Vincents Solicitors](https://vincentssolicitors.co.uk/?p=7545)
- Upside for landlords: a 2018 NPR report said landlords could charge higher rents for pop-ups than for long-term leases. [trade, 2018-10-31] [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2018/10/31/662436279/why-pop-up-shops-keeping-popping-up)
- US landlord and tenant friction: b8ta's CEO attributed the 2022 US exit to failed landlord negotiations. [trade] [Modern Retail, 2022-03-02](https://www.modernretail.co/retailers/b8ta-shutters-u-s-operations-after-failing-to-reach-a-deal-with-landlords/)
- Showfields' 2023 filing cited "rigid leases." Retail Dive reports landlords objected to its debtor-in-possession financing, which shows landlord leverage in insolvency. [trade] [RetailWire](https://retailwire.com/showfields-pioneer-of-immersive-retail-files-for-bankruptcy-amid-pandemic-woes/); [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/showfields-dtc-department-stores-closing/704453/)
- City workaround (SF, 2023): the city's economic development office and nonprofit SF New Deal intermediated between property owners and short-term tenants, with grants of $3,000 to $8,000 and up to three months' free rent (per search summary). Applications opened April 17, 2023; 850 applications by August 2023; 17 awardees opened late September 2023. [official / trade] [SF.gov](https://www.sf.gov/news--sf-new-deal-and-office-economic-and-workforce-development-oewd-launch-vacant-vibrant-bring-pop); [ABC7](https://abc7news.com/amp/post/san-francisco-vacant-to-vibrant-program-17-pop-ups-shops-coming-sf-buildings-retail-spaces-financial-district/13696576/)

### Inferences
- Short-term leases move risk from the tenant to the landlord (reinstatement, handovers, holdovers). Marketplaces that intermediate these deals still face the same landlord cost structure, which fits b8ta's landlord dispute and Showfields' lease complaints.
- The SF program needed public subsidy and intermediation to get landlords to participate, which suggests private marketplaces lacked enough landlord incentive. Single program, weak evidence.

### Gaps
- US-specific use clauses, exclusive-use rights, co-tenancy, and assignment or sublease restrictions: no US source retrieved.
- Appraisal effects of short-term rent on property valuations and lender covenants: no source found. Not asserted.
- Broker incentives on short-term deals (commission structure): no source found.
- ICSC commentary on pop-up leasing: PDF listed but not opened.
- NYC vacant-storefront programs: not searched.

## KQ4. Why venue software (Tripleseat, Perfect Venue) appears more durable than marketplaces

### Takeaway
The venue-software path survived by consolidating marketplaces into it. EventUp moved into Gather then Tripleseat, and VenueBook moved into Seated, a restaurant-rewards platform. This is consistent with software revenue being less dependent on booking volume, but no revenue or pricing data was gathered to test it. Perfect Venue was not checked.

### Cited Findings
- EventUp: acquired by Gather around 2018, then moved into Tripleseat after the March 2020 Vista merger. [trade] [FSR](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/industry-news/tripleseat-acquired-vista-equity-partners-merge-gather/); [EventUp blog, 2020-06-25](https://eventupblog.tripleseat.com/eventup-acquired-tripleseat)
- VenueBook: acquired by Seated, Aug 2020, which described event bookings as a third revenue stream for restaurants. [primary] [BusinessWire, 2020-08-18](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20200818005271/en/Seated-Announces-%2430M-in-Funding-and-the-Acquisition-of-VenueBook-to-Further-Expand-its-Rewards-Platform-in-Support-of-Restaurants)
- Tripleseat: Vista-owned; combined company operates from Concord, MA, with Atlanta office; investors include Vista's Endeavor Fund and Union Square Hospitality Group affiliate. [trade] [FSR](https://www.fsrmagazine.com/industry-news/tripleseat-acquired-vista-equity-partners-merge-gather/)
- Pricing signal (anecdotal): reviewers said software prices rose after the merger. [agg, anecdotal] [Secret reviews](https://www.joinsecret.com/tripleseat/reviews)
- Marketplace economics as reported: Splacer about 15% (lead, unverified); Bulletin 30% of sales plus rent (2017). Both are transaction- or sales-linked, unlike seat-based software.

### Inferences
- Software revenue accrues from venues whether or not a booking occurs, while marketplace revenue depends on booking volume and needs demand on both sides. The buyers chose software or restaurant platforms, which fits this reading. Not directly tested with data.
- Durability tracks backing: Vista (Gather and Tripleseat) and Insight Partners (Seated) are growth or PE investors. Marketplace survivors without comparable backing (Venuetastic, Splacer) did not last. Small sample.
- Tripleseat's later acquisitions (Attendease 2023, Merri 2024) show it continued to buy adjacent tools, but this is only in one summary.

### Gaps
- Perfect Venue: not researched (status, funding, ownership).
- Tripleseat and Gather revenue, customer counts, and current pricing: no primary source.
- Tripleseat Attendease (Aug 2023) and Merri (June 2024) acquisitions: no retrieved URL. [lead]
- Independent SaaS pricing evidence for venue software: none.

## KQ5. Founder post-mortems and investor commentary

### Takeaway
