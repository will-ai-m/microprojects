## Key Question 1: What is the Reynaga case, is it the cause of the filing, and are there other lawsuits?

### Takeaway
The best-supported trigger is the Reynaga negligence and premises-liability suit over a July 2024 staircase fall at a Chicago residence. The case rests on a single aggregator summary, so it needs court-record confirmation. Other Peerspace-named suits found are sparse (one 2018 New York premises case).

### Cited Findings
- July 2024: a guest (Ezequiel Reynaga, age 31 per aggregator) fell from the top of a staircase to the floor below at a Chicago residence listed on the platform and was left paraplegic. Sept 2024: he sued Peerspace and the space's owners and operators in Circuit Court of Cook County for negligence and premises liability. [A] Bondoro case summary, https://app.bondoro.com/preview/cases/Peerspace_Inc/case-summary (undated; quoted via search snippet)
- Per the same summary: every other defendant settled with Reynaga. Good-faith settlement talks with Peerspace had not succeeded. Trial was scheduled to begin Aug 31, 2026. Peerspace filed to pause the case and possibly facilitate a settlement. If no settlement, Peerspace plans to propose a plan that resolves Reynaga's claim. [A] same source.
- Company statement, as relayed by WhatNow: the filing "aims to resolve pending litigation" and does not reflect the health of the platform; the company expects to operate in the ordinary course. Statement date not shown in the search result (after Aug 30, 2026). [N] https://whatnow.com/news/trending/san-francisco-venue-rental-marketplace-with-45000-spaces-files-for-chapter-11-bankruptcy/
- Date check (my calculation): Aug 30, 2026 was a Sunday and Aug 31 was a Monday. The petition was therefore filed the day before the reported Monday trial start. [inference from reported dates]
- Conflict: a jobs-board page says a negligence trial "began the same day as the filing" and that liquidation would follow if creditors reject the plan. Its trial date conflicts with the Aug 31 date above, and I could not corroborate either claim. [U] https://simplify.jobs/c/Peerspace
- Other Peerspace-named suit found: Reed Yurman v. Ursula E. Pohl and Peerspace, Inc. (premises-liability negligence, New York County Supreme Court, filed Feb 2018). Listing shows disposed; the record was last refreshed about three years earlier. [A] https://trellis.law/case/36061/151507-2018/reed-yurman-v-ursula-e-pohl-peerspace-inc
- Searches for shootings or serious injuries at Peerspace bookings found no Peerspace-named suit. Comparable venue suits name venue operators or security firms, not booking platforms. Example: a Chicago 2023 Halloween-party shooting suit against a venue and its security firm. [N] https://hoodline.com/2024/02/chicago-halloween-party-venue-and-security-firm-face-negligence-lawsuit-following-mass-shooting-ordeal/ (context only, not Peerspace)
- Trust and safety, 2020: Peerspace's blog says pandemic closures of bars, clubs and event spaces led to more illegal or unauthorized parties on the platform. It then introduced a Social Events Policy. [P] https://www.peerspace.com/blog/social-events-policy (2020)

### Inferences
- The timing (petition the day before trial, with the company citing pending litigation) suggests the filing was litigation-driven. An automatic stay halts the trial, and a plan can resolve the claim. This is the most coherent explanation so far, but it rests on one aggregator chain.
- Whether Reynaga alone caused the distress is unknown. The aggregator's balance-sheet picture (no secured debt, small trade debt, mostly disputed or unliquidated claims) suggests the litigation is the main creditor claim. That argues against a debt-service collapse, but it is not verified.
- A single catastrophic-injury claim could be large relative to a company with roughly $10–15M of annual revenue (see KQ4). That is an inference about scale, not a sourced fact.

- The Reynaga name appears only in the Bondoro-derived summary. Searches combining Reynaga with Peerspace, Cook County or staircase returned nothing relevant.
- Whether the trial started, settled or was stayed on Aug 31: not found.
- Other lawsuits (wage, privacy or data, ADA, tax, host-guest disputes): none found in limited searches. Court indexes were not accessible, so this is not an exhaustive check.

## Key Question 3: Subchapter V eligibility, the 2026 debt limit, and what eligibility implies

### Takeaway
The Subchapter V debt cap in effect on Aug 30, 2026 was $3,424,000 (April 1, 2025 adjustment). Legislation to restore $7.5M had cleared at least one chamber and was reported to have passed both, but enactment is unconfirmed and sources conflict. A $10–50M liability range fitting under a $3.4M cap implies most debts are contingent, unliquidated or disputed, which matches the litigation profile.

### Cited Findings
- The §101(51D) Subchapter V limit rose to $3,424,000 on April 1, 2025, from $3,024,725. The temporary $7.5M ceiling expired June 21, 2024. [A] ABI, https://www.abi.org/member-resources/blogs?page=40 (headline "Subchapter V debt limit will increase to $3,424,000"); a Federal Register notice (90 FR 8941) was cited but not read.
- S. 3977 (Bankruptcy Threshold Adjustment Act of 2026) would make the $7.5M ceiling permanent and set a $2.75M combined Chapter 13 cap. It passed the Senate by unanimous consent on Aug 3, 2026 (sources differ, Aug 3 or Aug 4). [A] AFS, https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/congress-advances-permanent-75-million-subchapter-v-threshold-giving-more-small ; [A] Beancount, https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/10/subchapter-v-bankruptcy-debt-limit-2026-guide
- House companion H.R. 7730 passed the House by voice vote on Sept 16, 2026 (Bloomberg Law and AFS). An ABI post dated Sept 18 says the day before, a date conflict. [N] AFS, https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/bankruptcy-legislation-update-house-passes-bill-preserving-75-million ; [N] ABI, https://abi.org/node/340563
- Senate approved the House version Sept 28, 2026 (AFS); the latest item found says it awaited the president's signature. A separate mid-September status note says both chambers still needed identical text. Enactment is unconfirmed as of Oct 25, 2026. [N] AFS; [N] Bloomberg Tax, https://news.bloombergtax.com/bankruptcy-law/senate-approves-house-backed-small-business-bankruptcy-bill
- Peerspace's petition (Aug 30, 2026) predates any enactment reported in these sources. The $3.424M cap therefore applied at filing. [inference from timeline; see Inferences]

### Inferences
- Statutory framework (from general knowledge of 11 U.S.C. §§101(51D) and 1182(1); statute text not retrieved this session): the debt cap counts only noncontingent, liquidated debts, excluding insider or affiliate debts. Contingent, unliquidated and disputed claims do not count toward the cap.
- If Peerspace was eligible under $3.424M while listing $10–50M (or $16.8M) in total liabilities, most liabilities must be contingent, unliquidated or disputed. That fits the Reynaga tort claim plus other disputed claims described by Bondoro. This is the most coherent reading of the eligibility fact pattern. It is not a court finding.
- Eligibility is measured at the petition date, so later legislation would not change an already-filed case. This is general bankruptcy practice, not sourced here.

### Gaps
- Statute text and Federal Register notice not read directly.
- Enactment status of H.R. 7730 and S. 3977 as of Oct 25, 2026: unresolved. Check Congress.gov.
- Peerspace's actual noncontingent liquidated debt figure, from the schedules: not accessed.

## Key Question 5: Structural challenges (demand, disintermediation, trust and safety, insurance, competition, macro)

### Takeaway
Verified items are limited. Peerspace's own insurance is supplemental and modest: up to $1M of general liability per booking and a $25K property-damage guarantee in the US. Hosts and guests must carry primary insurance. Competitors (Giggster, Splacer, Tagvenue) claim lower take rates in marketing material. Demand, Google and AI-search dependence, repeat direct booking, booking frequency, host churn and macro conditions were not supported by any source I could access.

### Cited Findings
- Liability cover: every booking includes up to $1M supplemental general liability, applicable to bookings that comply with Peerspace's Services Agreement. [P] Peerspace help center, https://intercom.help/peerspace-108cf19aff8e/en/articles/10119116 (search snippet; fetch failed)
- Damage guarantee: up to $25,000 per booking for damage to furniture, floors or equipment. US venues only. [P] https://www.peerspace.com/legal/terms/property-damage-guarantee (search snippet)
- Primary insurance: hosts and guests must maintain primary insurance covering their activities; Peerspace's policy is supplemental. [P] Peerspace help center (snippet)
- Guest damage: hosts report damage within 3 days after the event, and Peerspace can bill the guest. Hosts can collect a refundable deposit before the booking. [P] Peerspace help center (snippet)
- Third-party claims about UK (£1M) and Canada (C$1M) limits, a 48-hour claim window and exclusions are unverified. [U] https://shunins.com/article/does-peerspace-have-insurance
- Fees today: host service fee of 15–20%, varying by location, earnings and hosting history. Guest processing fee is a percentage of the subtotal and falls as the subtotal rises. [P] https://intercom.help/peerspace-108cf19aff8e/en/articles/10119157 (snippet) ; [P] https://www.peerspace.com/legal/terms/fees-overview
- Take rate in 2017: 15% from hosts and 5% from renters. [N] VentureBeat 2017, https://venturebeat.com/business/peerspace-raises-11-million-to-provide-original-event-locations
- Competitor claims (marketing): Tagvenue says Peerspace charges a 20% service fee on the booking amount, and that Tagvenue charges venues 10% on events and 15% on meetings or productions with no planner fee. [M] https://www.tagvenue.com/blog/how-to-find-unique-filming-locations-tagvenue-peerspace-giggster-compared/ (a Tagvenue blog; the post carrying the 20% claim is not confirmed)
- Giggster: Santa Monica, founded 2016, reportedly takes about 15% from producers, per the Los Angeles Business Journal as quoted by Tagvenue. [M] same Tagvenue blog
- Other competitors: Splacer (New York or Brooklyn, founded 2014, also offers event planning); Inven lists Giggster, OfficeRiders and Splacer as Peerspace competitors. [A] https://www.inven.ai/company-lists/top-25-peerspace-competitors ; [A] https://www.cbinsights.com/company/splacer/alternatives-competitors
- Tagvenue scale: 22,000+ spaces across the UK, Ireland, Australia, Singapore, Canada and the US. [M] Tagvenue blog

### Inferences
- Insurance structure: Peerspace's per-booking cover is small relative to a catastrophic injury claim. Its own exposure therefore depends on host and guest primary insurance. If that cover is inadequate or disputed, a claim naming Peerspace may fall on its balance sheet. Not verified against the policy wording.
- Take-rate pressure: a 15–20% host fee plus a guest fee sits above the 10–15% commissions Tagvenue advertises. This could weigh on price competitiveness in venue-heavy segments. Based on competitor marketing only.
- All Inclusive Venues (catering and staff in one booking) moves Peerspace from listing marketplace toward service delivery. That typically raises operational, staffing and liability exposure. Not sourced.
