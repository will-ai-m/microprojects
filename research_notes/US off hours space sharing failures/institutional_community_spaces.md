# US Off-Hours Rental of Schools, Churches, and Public Facilities: Companies, Economics, and Barriers

> Research date: 2026-10-25. Scope: United States only. Non-US examples (Ontario, Canada) were found but excluded from findings; they are listed at the end for reference only.
>
> Method and limits: about 15 research calls, all WebSearch. I did not open any underlying page with WebFetch, so every fact below rests on a search-result summary plus the URL it came from. Treat the exact figures as unverified until checked against the primary page. Each finding carries its date where the source showed one, and "date not shown" where it did not. Vendor-reported numbers and single-source claims are flagged.

## Key Question 2: What legal and governance barriers apply to public schools and municipal facilities?

### Takeaway
California's Civic Center Act is the clearest documented legal constraint. A Facilities Dive summary says the 2012 expansion that let districts count a share of operating and maintenance costs lapsed, so districts appear limited to direct costs again. A bill (AB 503) to restore the broader recovery was described as pending; its current status is unverified. Wyoming enacted a cost-based rental fee cap effective July 1, 2026 (single aggregator source). District reports repeatedly show operational pressure from custodial staffing and from rates that cut demand or leave bookings unbilled. Equal-access, union, and background-check rules were not documented in this research.

### Cited Findings
- Original Civic Center Act text limited district rental fees to "direct costs," with exceptions for religious organizations and for events where organizers charge admission. Per Facilities Dive summary. [Facilities Dive, CA cost-recovery bill](https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/california-bill-would-expand-school-districts-cost-recovery-options-for-fa/748352/)
- 2012 expansion (SB 1404): districts could count a proportional share of operating, maintenance, and repair costs as direct costs. A 2012 Senate analysis estimated $50,000–$75,000 in General Fund cost to write regulations. [Senate Committee analysis, SB 1404](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/11-12/bill/sen/sb_1401-1450/sb_1404_cfa_20120524_095438_sen_comm.html)
- Per the Facilities Dive summary, a five-year extension passed, a second did not, and since January 1 (year not stated in the snippet) districts have been limited to the original direct-cost definition. [Facilities Dive](https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/california-bill-would-expand-school-districts-cost-recovery-options-for-fa/748352/)
- AB 503 would restore the broader cost recovery. Current status not verified. [Facilities Dive](https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/california-bill-would-expand-school-districts-cost-recovery-options-for-fa/748352/)
- Wyoming: a newly enacted law, effective July 1, 2026, caps district rental fees at the hourly cost of staff, equipment, utilities, and supplies, and does not alter existing contracts. Single aggregator source; verify against the Wyoming statute. [Citizen Portal](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/8086585/wyoming/legislative/committees/select-committees-task-force/select-water-committee/new-law-caps-school-facility-rental-fees-at-actual-costs-starting-july-1-2026)
- Chesterfield County Public Schools (VA), July 23, 2026 benchmarking review: CCPS facility rates are 8% above benchmarked divisions. A separate review of 2024–25 reservations estimated about $25,065 in possible additional revenue from bookings never invoiced. Government document. [Chesterfield County, 2026-07-23](https://www.chesterfield.gov/DocumentCenter/View/48390/Schools-Use-or-Rental-of-School-Facilities-July-23-2026-PDF)
- Conroe ISD (TX): trustees were shown rentals ran about $150,000 short between January and August of the prior year, with roughly $488,000 in income against nearly $639,000 in costs. Officials said the increases may have reduced demand. Article date not shown. [Houston Chronicle](https://www.chron.com/news/houston-texas/education/article/conroe-isd-tutoring-spaces-rental-texas-21257490.php)
- Litchfield Elementary School District: rental revenue rose about 46% (about $110,000) after a fee revamp, but hours used fell in most categories. Per Citizen Portal; cycle 2025–26 per summary. [Citizen Portal](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/6479915/district-reports-110000-revenue-gain-from-facility-rental-fee-changes-outstanding-balances-remain)
- Shakopee Public Schools (MN), January 2025: rentals grew every year since 2021, funding a $37,000 theater lighting upgrade and contributing to a $287,000 committed fund balance. Custodial staffing named as the pressure point; turf and outdoor spaces underused. [Citizen Portal](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/6363600/Minnesota/School-Boards/SHAKOPEE-PUBLIC-SCHOOL-DISTRICT/District-facilities-rentals-bring-rising-revenue-but-raise-staffing-and-scheduling-challenges)
- Souderton Area SD (PA), May 2026 proposal: move from per-event charges to hourly rates capped at six hours, with separate pricing for community and non-community groups and new staffing charges. [Citizen Portal](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/8027970/pennsylvania/school-districts/souderton-area-sd/district-proposes-hourly-system-for-facility-rentals-caps-and-new-fee-classes-to-limit-turf-wear-and-better-reflect-staffing-costs)
- Bellingham SD 501 (WA), fall 2026: elementary and middle gyms and fields rentable after 6:00 p.m. on weekdays; high school facilities after 7:00 p.m., unless school events are scheduled. [Bellingham SD](https://www.bellinghamschools.org/about/departments/operations/facility-rental-and-use)
- Newark USD (CA): school-related activities get priority; other use is first-come, first-served; submission three weeks before use. [Newark USD](https://www.newarkunified.org/departments/business-services/facility-rental-at-nusd)
- Sacramento City USD: nonprofit and youth groups with many district students pay nothing or very little. Per a district official. [SVVoice](https://www.svvoice.com/facility-use-fee-report-causes-uproar-possible-agnews-schools-names-debuted/)
- Cost recovery as a general California principle: a city fee study says municipal fees are generally capped at the estimated reasonable cost of the service. This is about city fees, not Civic Center Act charges. [City fee study, ggcity.org](https://ggcity.org/sites/default/files/development-processing-fee-study-building-and-planning-fees_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- If a district's fee is capped at direct cost, a vendor commission added on top raises an open question: is it part of the district's fee (and so capped), or is it outside the cap? No source answers this. It matters most for commission-based platforms in California.
- Cost-based caps (Wyoming, Souderton's proposal) push pricing toward hourly staff cost. That makes custodial and staff cost the binding constraint on after-hours use.
- The Conroe and Litchfield results suggest price increases can reduce hours used. Revenue gains may not last.

### Gaps
- Equal-access and nondiscrimination rules (can the district refuse some renters?), board policy examples, procurement thresholds, union or custodial overtime rules, background checks and child-safety requirements, and security: none retrieved. Suggested primary source: California Education Code, Civic Center Act provisions (section 38130 and following; section number not verified in this session).
- AB 503 current status and the year the lapse took effect: not verified.
- Wyoming statute text: not checked.
- Other states' civic-use statutes: not researched.

## Key Question 3: What barriers do churches and nonprofits face (UBIT, tax exemption, zoning, governance, staffing, mission)?

### Takeaway
Under the IRS guidance summarized by church-tax sources, rent from real property is generally excluded from unrelated business income, so UBIT risk is narrower than often assumed. The main triggers are debt-financed property, services provided along with the space, and rental of movable equipment such as sound systems or tables. Filing starts at $1,000 of gross unrelated income. Below-market rent to insiders carries excise-tax risk. Sources disagree on how aggressive the rule is. Property-tax exemption, zoning, denominational approval, and theological objections were not documented in this research.

### Cited Findings
- General IRS church guidance (as quoted by the UUA): income from rental of real property and incidental personal property is generally excluded from unrelated business income. [UUA](https://www.uua.org/node/103782)
- Debt exception: a mortgage or other debt on the property can make the rent unrelated debt-financed income. Land bought with debt and used for exempt purposes within 15 years may be exempt. [UUA](https://www.uua.org/node/103782)
- Services and equipment: personal services tied to a rental, and rental of movable equipment (sound systems, chairs, tables), can produce unrelated business income. [UUA](https://www.uua.org/node/103782); [Outreach Magazine](https://outreachmagazine.com/?p=43838)
- Filing thresholds: $1,000 or more gross unrelated income requires Form 990-T; expected tax of $500 or more requires quarterly estimated payments. [UUA](https://www.uua.org/node/103782)
- Expenses directly tied to the rental (utilities, custodial care) can offset the income, so net taxable income is often zero. [UUA](https://www.uua.org/node/103782)
- Mission-related tenant: rental to a group that furthers the church's mission may avoid UBIT. A 2002 IRS letter ruling on a public museum renting meeting space found no UBIT and no effect on exempt status. [Church Law & Tax](https://www.churchlawandtax.com/understand-taxes/income/irs-addresses-rental-of-meeting-space/)
- Below-market rent to insiders: excise tax exposure can reach up to 225% of the benefit value. [Church Law & Tax](https://www.churchlawandtax.com/understand-taxes/income/irs-addresses-rental-of-meeting-space/)
- Exempt status: rental usually does not cost the church its exemption if the activity is insubstantial. Neither Congress nor the IRS has defined "insubstantial." [Church Law & Tax](https://www.churchlawandtax.com/page/16/?id=59265)
- Conflict in sources: one homeschoolcpa.com post calls renting space a commercial activity that the IRS taxes by default. This is more aggressive than the IRS-guidance summaries above. The sources are consultant and denominational pages, not IRS primary text. [homeschoolcpa.com](https://homeschoolcpa.com/?p=6753)
- State sales tax on rentals may apply. Check the state department of revenue. [Outreach Magazine](https://outreachmagazine.com/resources/architecture-and-building/48242-renting-church-for-profit-business.html)
- Partners for Sacred Places' "Creating Spaces" research (Three City Arts Study; Austin, Baltimore, Detroit; supported in part by the Doris Duke Charitable Foundation) matched small and mid-size performing-arts groups with historic houses of worship that have spare space. Drexel's February 2016 item says it proposed a matching model. Partners' site says fieldwork began in 2014 and ran about 18 months. [Partners for Sacred Places](https://sacredplaces.org/consulting/shared-space-adaptive-reuse); [Drexel, 2016-02-08](https://drexel.edu/westphal/news-events/news/2016/february/2016-2-08-findingartsvenues); [Nonprofit Quarterly](https://nonprofitquarterly.org/shared-sanctuary-revisited-growing-trend-among-artists-work-historic-places-worship/)
- The report PDF itself was not retrieved, so its detailed findings are unverified.

### Inferences
- UBIT exposure depends on the bundle as much as on the rent. A marketplace that sells catering, staffing, AV, or cleanup alongside the space could create UBIT that bare space rental would not.
- Churches with recent debt-financed construction face a different UBIT position from debt-free churches.
- Renting to a denomination, a sister congregation, or a board member at below-market rates raises excise-tax risk.

### Gaps
- Property-tax exemption risk for commercial rental: state-by-state rules not researched.
- Zoning and use-permit barriers: no sources found.
- Denominational approval (trustees, presbyteries, bishops): no sources found.
- Volunteer staffing and labor for church rentals: no sources found. The only staffing source is the Shakopee custodial comment (public schools).
- Theological and mission objections to particular renters (alcohol, political or advocacy groups, LGBTQ groups): no sources found. This is a meaningful omission for the report.
- ChurchSpace-specific tax or insurance guidance: none found.

## Key Question 4: What are the insurance and certificate-of-insurance (COI) requirements, and how do platforms solve them?

### Takeaway
Churches and school districts typically require renters to provide a COI that names the owner as additional insured (some require named insured). Per-occurrence limits of about $1M are common in examples. Higher limits are required for high-risk events such as alcohol or minors. Facilitron says it handles COI collection. A Canadian diocese uses an instant-issue broker portal, which is non-US. No US platform-provided event-insurance product was verified.

### Cited Findings
- First Presbyterian Pittsburgh: the renter's certificate names the church as named insured. [FPCP](https://fpcp.org/facility-rental)
- Covenant Lutheran (search summary; source URL not confirmed): renter names the church as additional insured for non-church events; deadline two weeks before first use; annual renewal for continuing users.
- St. John Neumann Parish (search summary; source URL not confirmed): general liability not less than $1,000,000 per occurrence.
- Arlington Catholic Diocese facility-use agreement (rev. 2020): certificate due thirty days before the event. [Arlington Diocese](https://arlingtondiocese.org/risk-management/documents/renters-liability/01_facility_use_agreement_rev2020.pdf)
- UCC Magnolia facility-use agreement: user defends and indemnifies the church. [UCC Magnolia](https://uccmagnolia.org/ewExternalFiles/23-04%20UCC%20Facility%20Use%20Agreement.pdf)
- Some agreements require the renter's coverage to be primary and non-contributory, and include an indemnity clause. Pattern seen across the sample in search results; no single source confirmed.
- GuideOne certificate request form asks for insurer, policy expiration, limits, whether the renter has an umbrella policy, and planned activities (for example, dancing or power tools). [GuideOne](https://www.guideone.com/sites/default/files/2021-04/certificate_of_insurance_request_form_1.pdf)
- Diocese of Columbus offers its own renter coverage for $95 per event, plus a fee if alcohol is served (single search-summary source; URL not confirmed).
- Facilitron handles payment and COI collection (as stated on Facilitron-hosted district portals). [Santa Clara USD portal](https://www.facilitron.com/scusd95051)
- Non-US context only: Toronto Anglican Diocese sets $2M per occurrence for low-risk licensees and $5M for high-risk, where high-risk includes alcohol and minors. It issues COIs instantly through an online broker portal. [Toronto Anglican Diocese](https://www.toronto.anglican.ca/churchwarden-toolbox/insurance)

### Inferences
- Because every rental needs a COI, a platform that automates verification and additional-insured endorsements reduces owner friction. This is a likely advantage for a district-side platform like Facilitron. No source compared platforms on this point.
- Short-notice hourly rentals fit poorly with annual or thirty-day COI workflows. The Arlington and Covenant examples suggest friction for ad hoc renters, which favors marketplaces that bundle insurance at booking.

### Gaps
- No US source on platform-provided per-event insurance from ChurchSpace, Peerspace, Facilitron, or Skedda.
- Insurer appetite for church-rental exposure (for example, GuideOne or Church Mutual): not researched.
- District liability structures (self-insurance, joint powers authorities): not researched.

## Key Question 5: Why might Facilitron's model work better than marketplaces, and what are its weaknesses and criticisms?

### Takeaway
The best-supported explanation for Facilitron's footprint is that districts pay nothing up front for the software and get booking, payments, and COI handling in one district-facing workflow. The platform earns a commission on rentals (a 10% revenue fee in one Virginia contract). Its main public criticism is that the commission is passed to renters, raising costs for nonprofit and youth groups. Boards have objected. The $500M headline is described inconsistently across releases. No source directly compares Facilitron to failed competitors, so "why it succeeded where others did not" is inference.

### Cited Findings
- Virginia school division: Facilitron contract takes a 10% fee on revenue. Board members raised concern about higher costs for small nonprofits. A sample projection showed rates rising from about $23,000 to $32,000 before that fee (wording in summary is ambiguous). [Soar citizen portal](https://cpo.soar.com/articles/7970704/Virginia/School-board-approves-new-facility-rental-fee-schedule-over-nonprofit-concerns-101)
- Glendale Unified (CA; Crescenta Valley Weekly, date not shown): board members said the commission payments to Facilitron made fees "cost prohibitive" for community organizations. One member said, "I see no need to give that commission away." Staff projected 46 of 212 user groups would move to commercial rates. [Crescenta Valley Weekly](https://www.crescentavalleyweekly.com/?p=153294)
- Sacramento City USD: a district official acknowledged that the fee schedule caused confusion. Facilitron came out of a 2017 task force on booking and cost-recovery problems. [SVVoice](https://www.svvoice.com/facility-use-fee-report-causes-uproar-possible-agnews-schools-names-debuted/)
- Company-cited outcome: Mesa County Valley School District 51 (CO) saved over $1 million in one year by cutting HVAC use 24%, per the August 2025 release. Single-district, vendor-cited. [PR Newswire, 2025-08-26](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/facilitron-helps-public-schools-generate-over-half-a-billion-dollars-in-hidden-revenue-302539321.html)
- Lynwood USD (CA): new Facilitron system operational for all sites as of January 2026. [Lynwood USD](https://www.mylusd.org/departments/motf/facility-use-application)
- Visalia USD (CA): facility fees rise 10% effective July 1, 2026. Fort Worth ISD: new fee schedule applies to events on or after August 11, 2026. Volusia County Schools: rate update for reservations on or after July 1, 2026. These district fee changes are not attributed to Facilitron in the sources. [Visalia](https://www.facilitron.com/vusd93277); [Fort Worth ISD](https://www.facilitron.com/fwisd76107); [Volusia](https://www.facilitron.com/vcs32720)
- Third-party critique (undated): the $500M is cumulative transaction volume, and the platform takes 6–12% of external rentals. Not verified. [checkthat.ai](https://checkthat.ai/brands/facilitron)

### Inferences
- Why the model may outperform marketplaces (inference, not sourced): (1) with no upfront software fee, a district avoids a budget line item and possibly a software RFP, which removes a common approval hurdle; procurement thresholds were not checked. (2) The district keeps control of the calendar and policies, and the platform sits in front of existing facilities instead of recruiting listings. (3) Compliance functions (COI, payments, fee schedules) are bundled. (4) The vendor's incentive is volume across many districts.
- Weaknesses (partly inferred): (a) commissions raise renter costs, which conflicts with cost-recovery and nonprofit-access goals, and the Civic Center Act question is open; (b) headline metrics are self-reported and inconsistent; (c) the commission base depends on district fee schedules that boards set and revise; (d) visible board pushback at contract review; (e) no evidence on renter experience or on renewal rates.
- Marketplace models (ChurchSpace, Peerspace) take fees from both sides and need liquidity on both sides. No outcome data for either was found, so this is inference only.

### Gaps
- No renewal, churn, or contract-loss data for Facilitron.
- No head-to-head comparison with failed competitors. I found no documented failures of other school-rental platforms in this research.
- Facilitron's actual rate card or primary contract not reviewed. The 6–12% and 10% figures come from one aggregator and one district contract.
- Whether a vendor commission counts toward a California district's capped "fee": unknown.

## Key Question 6: How large is the US opportunity?

### Takeaway
There are roughly 350,000 to 375,000 US congregations, depending on source. Most are small: about 90% have 250 or fewer attendees (2020 data), so per-congregation rental potential is likely modest and concentrated in larger congregations. The count of US K-12 schools was not verified in this research. District rental revenue examples range from tens of thousands to roughly $1 million a year. Facilitron claims 10,000+ schools and colleges.

### Cited Findings
- About 350,000 US faith communities, per the 2020 Faith Communities Today (FACT) report as covered by Good Faith Media. [Good Faith Media](https://goodfaithmedia.org/most-u-s-faith-communities-are-small-with-declining-attendance/)
- A range of 350,000 to 375,000 US congregations of all traditions. [Baptist News Global](https://baptistnews.com/article/largest-ever-u-s-congregational-survey-confirms-what-church-consultants-have-been-telling-you/)
- About 373,000, attributed to the Hartford Institute by a church-marketing blog. Secondhand; verify with Hartford. [ReachRight Studios](https://reachrightstudios.com/blog/how-many-churches-in-america/)
- FACT 2020: representative results from 15,278 congregations in more than 80 denominations. About 90% of congregations have 250 attendees or fewer. Typical attendance fell from 137 (2000) to 65 (2020). Data collected early 2020, before the pandemic. [Good Faith Media](https://goodfaithmedia.org/most-u-s-faith-communities-are-small-with-declining-attendance/); [Hartford FACT 2020 article](https://hirr.hartfordinternational.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/A-Portrait-of-the-2020-Faith-Communities-Today-Study-Article-Theology-Today.pdf)
- Public district revenue examples (US): Shakopee (MN), rentals funding a $287,000 committed fund balance, January 2025 [Citizen Portal](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/6363600/Minnesota/School-Boards/SHAKOPEE-PUBLIC-SCHOOL-DISTRICT/District-facilities-rentals-bring-rising-revenue-but-raise-staffing-and-scheduling-challenges); Litchfield Elementary, about $110,000 increase after fee revamp [Citizen Portal](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/6479915/district-reports-110000-revenue-gain-from-facility-rental-fee-changes-outstanding-balances-remain); Conroe ISD, about $488,000 income against $639,000 cost in Jan–Aug of prior year [Houston Chronicle](https://www.chron.com/news/houston-texas/education/article/conroe-isd-tutoring-spaces-rental-texas-21257490.php); Chesterfield County, about $25,065 of possible unbilled revenue in 2024–25 [Chesterfield County, 2026-07-23](https://www.chesterfield.gov/DocumentCenter/View/48390/Schools-Use-or-Rental-of-School-Facilities-July-23-2026-PDF).
- Church revenue claim: up to $100,000 a year for Texas churches using ChurchSpace (company-supplied; unverified). [UrbanGeekz, 2025-05](https://urbangeekz.com/2025/05/churchspace-1-2m-airbnb-for-churches/)
- Facilitron platform reach: 10,000+ US K-12 schools and colleges (company-stated). [Facilities Dive](https://www.facilitiesdive.com/news/facilitron-school-rental-platform-facilities-management/717477/)

### Inferences
- Congregational demand is likely concentrated in the larger congregations, given the attendance distribution. Inference from the FACT figures.
- School-side opportunity is larger in count but limited by district policy, custodial staffing, and fee caps (for example, California and Wyoming).
- Illustrative arithmetic, not a finding: if 10% of about 350,000 congregations each earned $10,000 a year from rentals, the church segment would be about $350M a year. The 10% and $10,000 inputs have no sourced basis.
