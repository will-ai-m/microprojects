# US On-Demand and Flexible Workspace Businesses Built on Other Owners' Space: Failures, Survivors, and Economics

**Method and caveats.** This run used 15 tool calls, all WebSearch. No page was opened in full and WebFetch was not used, so nearly every fact below comes from a search snippet or a search-summary paraphrase. Items tagged [summary-only] were seen only in a search summary and need checking against the primary article before publication. Source types: [primary] = company or issuer press release or company site; [trade press] = industry or business outlet; [aggregator] = Wikipedia, CB Insights, Preqin, Fox News citing Crunchbase, and similar. Dates are given where the snippet showed one; otherwise "date not in snippet." No development after January 2026 was found for any company. The user-supplied Breather figure (~$114M to $123M) was not confirmed; search results gave ~$122M (BetaKit summary) and ~$127M (The Logic citing PitchBook).

## 1. Company-by-company facts: model, markets, funding, pricing, owner pay, outcome

### Takeaway
The clearest failure is Breather, a leasing-based room operator that spent nearly all its venture money, faced landlord litigation and lease abandonment in 2020, and sold its listings platform to Industrious in May 2021 for a reported US$3M cash price. Two restaurant-based models were absorbed or sold: Spacious (acquired by WeWork; closure set for Dec 31, 2019) and KettleSpace (sold its physical workspace business to WorkChew, April 2022). The survivors with the most visible post-2020 activity are asset-light: LiquidSpace (marketplace commission plus enterprise software, with 2025 and January 2026 launches, company-reported) and ResortPass (hotel amenity day passes, $26M Series B, November 2022). Upflex, Codi, Croissant, Gable and WeWork All Access show no shutdown in my searches, but I found no 2025 or 2026 status for any of them.

## 2. Was the leasing or balance-sheet model the killer? COVID-19 and remote or hybrid shifts

### Takeaway
Leasing looks like an amplifier of the COVID shock rather than a sole cause. Breather's documented failure involved rent liabilities and lease terminations, while asset-light operators kept operating or were bought. But I found no source that directly compares the leasing and marketplace unit economics, and operator-run restaurant models (Spacious, KettleSpace) also ended in sale or closure.

### Cited Findings
- Breather's landlord dispute: Pink Stone Capital claimed $79,000 in unpaid rent after terminating a SoHo lease; Breather had not vacated — [Allwork](https://allwork.space/2020/12/breather-sued-by-new-york-city-landlord/) [trade press, summary-only]
- Breather's CEO reportedly wanted to rebuild around technology rather than leasing spaces — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/breathers-us-uk-subsidiaries-reportedly-file-for-insolvency/) [trade press, summary-only]
- COVID-19 "hit a company already in financial trouble" (BetaKit, 2020) — same source, summary-only
- KettleSpace's sale of its physical business was attributed to the pandemic lull in office demand — [BusinessWire, Apr 26, 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220426005505/en/) [primary, press release; the pandemic link comes from a summary]
- Post-pandemic capital went to asset-light operators: WeWork led $30M into Upflex (June 2022) — [Verdantix](https://www.verdantix.com/insights/blogs/wework-invests-30m-into-upflex-to-capture-the-growing-opportunities-from-dispersed-hybrid-working-patterns); ResortPass raised $26M (Nov 2022) — [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20221130005386/en/ResortPass-Raises-26-Million-Series-B-for-Hotel-Amenity-Access); Gable raised $12M (Feb 2023) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/28/gable-series-a)
- Hybrid demand shape: Codi's 35% of customers leasing fewer than five days per week, and its alternating-day shared offices — [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/coworking/san-fran-flex-office-startup-is-like-the-timeshare-for-office-116007) [trade press, summary-only]
- 2025 enterprise demand: LiquidSpace reports enterprise flex adoption up ~170% year over year (company-reported, Oct 2025) — [summary-only; source not isolated]

### Inferences
- Fixed-cost leased inventory with no offsetting revenue failed under a demand shock. Asset-light models survived. Breather and KettleSpace both fit this, but the sample is tiny.
- Hybrid work moved demand toward enterprise-managed booking and fractional team seats (LiquidSpace, Codi) rather than by-the-hour rooms for individuals. This is inference, not a sourced finding.

### Gaps
- No unit-economics data (take rates vs. rent margins, utilization by room type).
- No founder or investor quote attributing failure specifically to the leasing model.
- No data on 2021 to 2026 utilization for these models.

## 3. Demand-side problems: willingness to pay, frequency, enterprise sales, substitutes, utilization

### Takeaway
My search surfaced price points but no utilization, frequency or conversion data. The consumer price points tested ($29 day pass, $99 to $129 per month for Spacious; $39 to $299 per month for Croissant) were not shown to hold up, and no source documents low willingness to pay or long enterprise sales cycles for these operators. Leisure day passes (ResortPass) and enterprise booking (LiquidSpace) appear more stable than by-the-hour work rooms, but this is inference.

### Cited Findings
- Spacious: $29 day pass; $99 to $129 per month (2018) — [Popup City](https://popupcity.net/spacious-turns-new-yorks-empty-restaurants-into-co-working-spaces) [trade press, summary-only]
- Croissant: $39 for 10 hours to $299 unlimited (2016) — [BuiltIn NYC](https://www.builtinnyc.com/articles/croissant-classpass-coworking) [trade press, summary-only]
- Codi: 35% of customers lease fewer than five days a week — [Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/coworking/san-fran-flex-office-startup-is-like-the-timeshare-for-office-116007) [trade press, summary-only]
- ResortPass: consumer leisure day passes at hotel pools, with ~900 to 2,000 hotels — [Hotel Technology News](https://hoteltechnologynews.com/2022/12/resortpass-lands-26-million-to-expand-its-hotel-pool-and-amenities-access-platform/) [trade press]
- LiquidSpace: its 2025 products target enterprise and federal buyers (Team Connect, GSA pilot) — [LiquidSpace press](https://resources.liquidspace.com/liquidspace-team-connect-press-release) [primary, company-reported]

### Inferences
- Consumer by-the-hour work demand is thin at these price points. Leisure passes and enterprise booking appear to have had the clearer purchase reason. Inference only.
- Enterprise sales cycles are a plausible constraint on the enterprise-facing pivots, but I found no source documenting one.

### Gaps
- No utilization, frequency, or conversion data for any company.
- No documented enterprise sales-cycle length.
- No source on preference for home, coffee shops or WeWork, although Codi's shared-office model suggests some members wanted fixed seats.

## 4. Supply-side problems: quality, owners pulling inventory, building rules, hotel revenue management, cannibalization

### Takeaway
Supply churn and landlord disputes are documented. Building rules, hotel revenue-management resistance and cannibalization are not documented in anything I found.

### Cited Findings
- Croissant's co-founder described frequent space churn (openings and closings several times a month) — [Technical.ly, Dec 5, 2017](https://technical.ly/2017/12/05/is-the-coworking-boom-over/) [trade press, quote as paraphrased in summary]
- Breather's landlord-termination dispute and abandoned leases (2020) — [Allwork](https://allwork.space/2020/12/breather-sued-by-new-york-city-landlord/) [trade press, summary-only]
- Spacious used revenue-share with restaurant owners and provided its own staffing, Wi-Fi and music — [Popup City](https://popupcity.net/spacious-turns-new-yorks-empty-restaurants-into-co-working-spaces) [trade press, summary-only]
- Codi ran background checks on hosts and insured them against damage — [NFX](https://nfx.com/post/3-reasons-why-nfx-invested-in-codi) [investor post, summary-only]

### Inferences
- Supply is structurally unstable. Partners (restaurants, homes, landlords) can exit with little notice, which hurt inventory reliability.
- Operator-run models (Spacious, Codi) carried quality-control costs that marketplaces did not.

### Gaps
- Building rules (after-hours HVAC charges, access control, landlord consent, insurance) — no source found.
- Hotel revenue-management resistance — no source found. ResortPass's SaaS-plus-commission model is one relevant lever, but I did not find hotel-side commentary.
- Cannibalization of owners' own walk-in or direct business — no source found.
- Quality inconsistency complaints from members — no source found.

## 5. What survivors did differently

### Takeaway
The surviving or sold models share three traits: they did not carry the room lease, they earned a commission or software fee, and they sold to a larger flex operator or moved toward enterprise and leisure buyers. Survival here is not proof of profitability; no survivor's financials were found.

### Cited Findings
- LiquidSpace: marketplace with ~10% commission (reported) and host subscriptions; strategic investors were landlords and operators (CBRE, Avison Young, GPT, Steelcase) — [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/how-this-company-is-helping-businesses-make-the-most-of-excess-office-space.print) [trade press]; [Xconomy](https://legacy.xconomy.com/?p=285492) [trade press]
- LiquidSpace 2025 to 2026: enterprise software and federal pilot — [LiquidSpace press](https://resources.liquidspace.com/press-release-gsa-fedworkplace) [primary, company-reported]
