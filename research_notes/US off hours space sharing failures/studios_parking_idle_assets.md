# US Off-Hours Idle-Space Monetization: Winners, Losers, and Why (Studios, Parking, Idle Assets)

As of 2026-10-25.

**Method and confidence.** All facts below come from WebSearch result snippets and aggregator summaries gathered in this session (16 searches). No full pages were fetched, so every figure is unverified against primary sources until checked. Dates are the dates given in the source or search summary; where a date is unknown it is marked as such.

**Tags:** [primary] = company page, press release, or official filing (as reported; the filing itself was not opened). [news] = press report. [agg] = aggregator, vendor, or consumer guide (Crunchbase, PitchBook, Tracxn, LeadIQ, Tagvenue, etc.). [single] = single-source claim, or claim with conflicting figures across sources.

## Key Question 1: Company-by-company profiles (model, US footprint, funding, scale, take rate, outcome)

### Takeaway
The clearest documented failure is REEF Technology (parking garages plus ghost kitchens): 750 layoffs in May 2022, kitchen closures in 2022 to 2023, and a 2023 pivot to licensing its tech. Durable signals exist for SpotHero (multi-round funding through 2019, no later round found), Bounce ($19M Series B, Nov 2024), ResortPass (Marriott global agreement, May 27, 2026), and Fever (valued above $1B in 2022). Swimply, Sniffspot, Neighbor, and Giggster raised money or have listed inventory, but I could not verify current scale or status for any of them after 2023. Take rates were reported only for Sniffspot (22%, aggregator summary) and Giggster (19% host commission per Tagvenue; ~15% producer fee per Giggster's own claim). None were found for SpotHero, ResortPass, Swimply, Bounce, or Fever.

## Key Question 2: What did the successes have in common?

### Takeaway
The stronger signals in this session (Bounce's 2024 round, ResortPass's Marriott deal, Fever's valuation, SpotHero's multi-round history) share a recurring need or a demand-creating platform, plus supply that is already operated by someone else (hotels, shops, existing parking). The evidence is thin, so the common-factor claims below are inferences.

### Cited Findings
- [news, 2026-05-27] Marriott uses ResortPass to sell day-use amenity access. Supply is hotel-owned; that the hotel carries the operating risk is my inference. [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/05/27/marriott-signs-resortpass-deal-why-hotels-are-pushing-to-sell-more-than-rooms/)
- [news, 2024-11-12] Bounce ($19M Series B) books luggage storage in partner shops and hotels, so partner sites supply the space (who carries operating risk is my inference). [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/11/12/bounce-grabs-19m-to-expand-its-traveller-convenience-network/)
- [company claim] Bounce: 13,000+ partner sites; revenue about 20x since Series A. Company claims, unverified.
- [agg/news, 2022] Fever runs the Candlelight concert series, sold as a recurring ticketed experience (creation history not verified here). Fever valued above $1B in 2022. [single-source-ish]
- [news/agg, 2014 to 2021] SpotHero and ParkWhiz/Arrive operate high-frequency, need-driven parking marketplaces. ParkWhiz/Arrive merged with FlashParking in 2021. SpotHero's post-2019 status is unverified.
- [agg] Sniffspot's 22% take rate and $2M host liability policy (one source) show that insurance is part of the platform offer. [single]
- [news, 2024-07-26] Host anecdote: a dog-park host earned enough from Sniffspot to fund a fourth site. Anecdotal. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2024/07/26/backyard-side-hustle-renting-out-land-as-private-dog-park-sniffspot.html) [single]

### Inferences
- High-frequency or already-demanded use cases (parking, luggage storage, day-use of hotel amenities) appear to outlast novelty use cases. This is inferred from which companies still show activity; no usage data supports it.
- Operator-as-supplier models (hotels in ResortPass, shops in Bounce) shift operating and zoning risk to a party already licensed for the activity. Residential owners (Swimply, Sniffspot) carry the risk themselves, which fits the regulatory record in KQ4.
- Demand-creating platforms (Fever, Sofar) must sell tickets, not just list space. Sofar's artist-pay disputes suggest the marketplace economics depend on thin, unpaid or low-paid supply.
- Insurance is both a product feature (Sniffspot $2M policy) and a regulatory lever (Carmel's draft $1M requirement). Whether Swimply's insurance is a competitive advantage is not established.

### Gaps
- No utilization, frequency, or unit-economic data for any winner. "High frequency" claims are inferred only.
- Swimply's insurance program is not verified; the brief's claim is unconfirmed.
- Owner-earns-without-presence claim for Swimply, Sniffspot, and Bounce is only partly supported (Sniffspot anecdote only).

## Key Question 4: US legal and regulatory fights

### Takeaway
The documented fights concentrate on residential backyard pool rentals (Swimply-type). These include enforcement in Minnesota, records demands in Rockland County NY, and local proposals or bans in Carmel, Montgomery County MD, San Jose, and Denver. I found no outcome for most proposals. No fights were found for hotel amenity passes, parking, or dog parks.

### Cited Findings
- [news, 2024-12-16] Minnesota: Swimply hosts challenged the state Department of Health's enforcement, calling it an unpromulgated, invalid rule ("shadow rulemaking"). [Minnesota Lawyer](https://minnlawyer.com/2024/12/16/pool-owners-allege-shadow-rulemaking/)
- [conflict] Penalty size is disputed: one source cites $10,000 non-compliance fines; Swimply cites "thousand-dollar fines." Not resolved.
- [news, 2025-01-05] Rockland County NY: the county sought Swimply's records (names, addresses, pool locations, transaction records from June 2018 to present). Swimply says the county lacks jurisdiction. [Rockland County Business Journal](https://rcbizjournal.com/2025/01/05/rockland-county-goes-after-swimplys-records-to-crack-down-on-backyard-pool-rentals/)
- [conflict] A search summary dates the county subpoena to 2022. The article's date is 2025. Unresolved.
- [news, undated; single] Carmel: a draft ordinance would limit private pool rental hours to 10 a.m. to 8 p.m., cap groups at 10, and require at least $1M in liability insurance. Which Carmel (state) is not stated in the snippet. [AOL](https://www.aol.com/carmel-ordinance-could-pour-cold-093743860.html) Outcome unknown.
- [news, 2023-05-30; single] Montgomery County MD: officials weighed formal rules for short-term pool rentals. Attribution to this source not confirmed. [Legally Sociable](https://legallysociable.com/2023/05/30/regulation-coming-for-renting-out-suburban-backyard-swimming-pools/) Outcome unknown.
- [news, 2022] San Jose: private pool rental not permitted under city code as of 2022; a resident is pushing to change that. [Patch](https://patch.com/california/campbell/why-renting-your-pool-illegal-san-jose)
- [single, no source link] Denver: a city spokesperson says residential pools cannot be used commercially.
- [single, no source link] Several jurisdictions (San Jose, New Jersey towns, Wisconsin) have tried over the past three years to ban such rentals or apply public-pool standards to private pools.
- [news, undated] Host-side article, title only (not read): "I listed my pool on Swimply. Here's what I found." [PoolPro](https://poolpromag.com/i-listed-my-pool-on-swimply-heres-what-i-found/)

### Inferences
- The enforcement logic is public-pool health standards (inspection, lifeguard, and water-quality rules) plus short-term-rental and neighborhood concerns. Insurance and hour or guest caps are the likely next compliance tools (Carmel draft).
- Commercially zoned inventory (hotels, shops) is less exposed to these rules. This is an inference.

### Gaps
- Outcomes of Carmel, Montgomery County, Minnesota, Rockland County, and San Jose remain unknown.
- HOA rules, Sniffspot ordinance status, and REEF health-code citation details not researched.
- No liability-incident reports for any platform found.

## Key Question 5: Gyms and studios; do owners rent off-hours, which platforms exist, and why have they not scaled?

### Takeaway
Off-hours rental of gyms and studios exists on several platforms: Giggster, Peerspace, Subler (facility rentals for schools and nonprofits), and Wellyx (gym-side software). I found no funding, scale, or failure data for any of them. The only usage figure is a vendor claim (boutique studios at about 37% capacity, citing ClassPass). Whether owners rent at scale is not established.

### Cited Findings
- [company pages] Giggster lists gyms and fitness studios by the hour in Brooklyn, Austin, Los Angeles, NYC, and San Diego, mostly for filming and events. Its LA page says weekday and late-night bookings are generally cheaper. [Giggster](https://giggster.com/find/los-angeles--ca/fitness-studio)
- [company resource] Peerspace lists gyms and yoga studios rented by the hour, with no membership required. [Peerspace](https://www.peerspace.com/resources/rent-fitness-space-by-the-hour/)
- [vendor] Subler: school and nonprofit facilities, including gym, yoga, dance, and martial arts listings, with bookings from early morning to late evening. [Subler](https://www.getsubler.com/)
- [vendor] Wellyx: gym owners rent out personal-training rooms, dance rooms, and recovery suites, in increments from minutes to full days. Yoga, Pilates, and dance studios can rent class spaces to outside instructors during off-peak hours. [Wellyx](https://wellyx.com/features/facility-rental/)
- [vendor, citing ClassPass; secondary citation unverified] The average boutique fitness studio runs at about 37% capacity. [Wellyx](https://wellyx.com/features/facility-rental/)
- Giggster listing example: a Brooklyn martial arts school opens its room to outside users during off hours. [Giggster](https://giggster.com/find/brooklyn--ny/fitness-studio)

### Inferences
- Likely reasons for limited scale (hypotheses, not sourced in this session): owner liability and insurance for outside instructors or renters; membership or lease restrictions; zoning and occupancy limits; equipment and cleaning access; and marketplace fees that are large relative to small per-hour studio revenue. The report writer should test these, not state them as findings.
- Gym and studio rental appears to be a sideline on general venue marketplaces (Giggster, Peerspace) rather than a standalone category with its own funding.

### Gaps
- No funding, valuation, or take-rate data for Subler, Wellyx, or Peerspace's fitness category.
- No owner survey or utilization data beyond the one vendor claim.
- No gym or studio platform failures found.
- Music rehearsal and photo studio platforms not researched beyond Giggster and Peerspace.
